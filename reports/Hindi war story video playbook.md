# Rebuild Badgam as a 12-minute Hindi film

The best video the evidence supports is a **roughly 12-minute, 1,500-word Hindi film**. It opens cold on the last stand at Badgam on 3 Nov 1947, then tells the story in order. It is built from four visual layers: animated maps, ink-and-watercolour illustrated frames with parallax, a real archival portrait, and kinetic type. It is voiced with Sarvam Bulbul v3 "Anand", generated one sentence per request with silences added in the edit. From it you cut **15 numbered Shorts/Reels**, released on a schedule pegged to **Army Day (Fri 15 Jan 2027), Republic Day (Tue 26 Jan) and the birth anniversary (Sun 31 Jan)**. The niche is open. No Somnath Sharma video has passed 1M views. The best long-form, a 2D-animation film from an 11.6K-subscriber channel, has **226.5K views**. The 35M-subscriber Lallantop got only 19K–51K on the same story, so packaging and format count for more than channel size ([DocoHidden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kcHs3CH5po8); [Lallantop E330](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vi1WWOVsFOI)). Two facts in the earlier 90-second baseline need fixing:

- **The "Bahadur" rescue story rests on a single non-scholarly source.** Drop it.
- **"About 1,000 raiders" overstates the most-cited figure.** CLAWS gives 1,000 as the strength the raiders *planned to gather*. The usual estimate is **about 700**.

Throughout this report, a claim with an inline link is sourced from the research notes. Anything marked **(inference)** is our own recommendation built on those sources. Most platform benchmarks come from third-party blogs, not from YouTube or Meta, and are flagged as such.

## The baseline's two history errors, and a safe fact base

The history notes rest on search summaries, because full-page fetches were blocked. Even so, the core biography is consistent across sources. Somnath Sharma was born **31 Jan 1923 at Dadh, Kangra**. He was commissioned on **22 Feb 1942 into the 19th Hyderabad Regiment, later 4 Kumaon**, and was mentioned in despatches in the Arakan ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma); [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/73888/major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-recipient/)). He went to Kashmir with his **left hand in plaster from a hockey injury**. Cardozo calls it an "arm fracture"; one blog says it was the right arm ([ThePrint/Cardozo](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/ambushed-injured-in-kashmir-how-major-somnath-sharma-earned-a-param-vir-chakra/185018/)).

On 3 Nov, A and D Companies of 4 Kumaon, reportedly with a company of 1 Kumaon, patrolled Badgam. The other companies began falling back around 1400 hrs. At **about 1435 hrs D Company came under fire from houses in the village** and was hit from three sides ([Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/); [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)). Sharma was killed by a mortar explosion. With him died Subedar Prem Singh Mehta and about 20 other ranks. A 1 Kumaon relief company and then 1 Punjab moved up to block the approach to the airfield ([Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)).

His Param Vir Chakra was the **first ever awarded, gazetted 21 June 1950**. The medal had been instituted on 26 Jan 1950 with effect from 15 Aug 1947 ([Wikipedia: PVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Param_Vir_Chakra)).

The two baseline claims fail against this record. The orderly "Bahadur" carried to safety in Burma appears only on [esamskriti](https://www.esamskriti.com/e/History/Great-Indian-Leaders/Major-SOMNATH-SHARMA--1.aspx). No other source names him or links the rescue to the mention in despatches. On raider numbers, the CLAWS paper says the lashkar arrived in small groups and **planned to attack once about 1,000 had gathered**. The figure most often given for the actual force is **about 700**. Popular accounts say 500, and Wikipedia gives "500–1,000" ([CLAWS, Mallya 2015](https://archive.claws.co.in/images/journals_doc/95557802_SameerMallya.pdf); [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)). ## Plaster, 50 yards and the Gita pages carry the story

The competitor data shows which story elements win. Every Somnath Sharma video that broke out puts **one vivid physical detail and a stake** in its title:

- "दुश्मन सिर्फ 50 गज दूर था" took **595.6K** views on a 74K channel ([TSP Vichar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kQG-nGLJXd4)).
- "Ek Toota Hua Haath… Kashmir Bacha Liya" took 226.5K ([DocoHidden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kcHs3CH5po8)).
- "टूटे हाथ से लड़ने वाले मेजर" took **82.5K in two days on a 373-subscriber channel**, posted on 7 Nov 2026, just after the anniversary ([रणगाथा](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kTzN7-XMCzs)).

Generic "Biography" and "Ep 1:" titles do worse even on large channels ([CREATIVE CONCEPTS Ep 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ifAHjw9a-KU)). The open angles are a well-sourced deep dive (the radio message, D Company, the airfield stakes, the Khanolkar link) and the **NCERT Class 8 chapter "A Tale of Valour"**. Student searches for that chapter currently find only 40–60 minute lectures, yet explainers on it have reached 193K ([English Chatterbox](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9828g5q2ksk); [mycbseguide](https://mycbseguide.com/blog/a-tale-of-valour-major-somnath-sharma-and-the-battle-of-badgam/)).

The structure follows YouTube's own retention logic. Studio's "Intro" metric is the share of viewers still watching at **30 seconds**, and YouTube reads a high figure as the opening matching the title and thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)). The leaked MrBeast memo asks for a re-engagement point at about 3 minutes ([OMR](https://omr.com/de/daily/mrbeast-doc-pdf-leak)). Open loops must be closed, or the content reads as clickbait ([Jay Acunzo](https://jayacunzo.com/blog/open-loops-a-simple-technique-to-make-your-stories-more-gripping)).

**(Inference)** The film therefore opens in the middle of the last stand and stops on an unresolved line. It then goes back to Dadh and plants two loops: "what was in his pocket" and "the medal, and the family it would touch". Each battle stage ends on "लेकिन…". The film returns to the cold-open image at the climax, and the Gita pages and the Khanolkar link close the two planted loops in the aftermath. The order of the outline below is **inference**. The facts in it carry the sourcing given in the previous section. Word counts assume **about 130 words per minute**, the middle of the inferred 120–140 wpm range for dramatic Hindi. That figure comes from a 150 wpm "normal" pace and a Hindi voice-over seller's rate of about 125 words per minute ([NarrationBox](https://narrationbox.com/tools/words-to-minutes-audio-calculator); [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/services/product/video-audio-a-perfect-indian-english-hindi-voice-over-1365533901786755072)).

**(Inference)** Script craft for Hindi narration:

- **Sentence length:** average 8–14 words; 4–8 in action passages. Nested "जो… वह…" clauses go at most one level deep.
- **Register:** everyday Hindustani (लड़ाई, हमला, गोली, जवान) rather than heavy Sanskritised words.
- **English military terms:** keep them, but write them in Devanagari so the TTS says them consistently (कंपनी, बटालियन, मोर्टार, रेडियो).
- **Numbers and dates:** spell them out in words ("तीन नवंबर उन्नीस सौ सैंतालीस"), because TTS engines can read digits in English. Test this early.
- **Address and tense:** "आप" for direct address; present tense from beat 6 on.
- **Dialogue:** none invented. Dialogue appears only where a source records it.
- **Refrain:** a Kumaon war cry could serve as a refrain, but the regiment's actual cry was not verified in the notes. Do not use one until it is.
- **Where sources disagree** on a number, say so in one line. This builds trust, which is the lesson from the criticism of selective framing in Dhruv Rathee-style explainers ([Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-research-like-dhruvrathee-script-like-dhruv-rathee-by-tube-sensei)).

A sample cold open in that register: "तीन नवंबर, उन्नीस सौ सैंतालीस। दोपहर के ढाई बजे के बाद का वक़्त। श्रीनगर के पास… बडगाम। अचानक, गाँव के घरों से गोलियाँ चलने लगती हैं। एक मेजर, जिसके बाएँ हाथ पर प्लास्टर चढ़ा है, रेडियो उठाता है— 'दुश्मन सिर्फ पचास गज दूर है।' उस संदेश के बाद जो हुआ… उसने इस चौबीस साल के अफ़सर को भारत का पहला परमवीर बना दिया।"

## Anand's voice needs sentence-level takes and editor silences

The sourced facts on Sarvam Bulbul v3 are thin. They come from search summaries of integration docs, because docs.sarvam.ai was blocked. v3 exposes **pace from 0.5 to 2.0 and temperature from 0.01 to 1.0**. Per the Pipecat and Mastra docs, it does **not support pitch or loudness**, which were v2 features ([Mastra](https://mastra.ai/reference/voice/sarvam); [Pipecat](https://docs.pipecat.ai/api-reference/server/services/tts/sarvam)). Sarvam's own product page claims pitch control, so the sources conflict ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/text-to-speech)). Segmind suggests keeping pace near 1.0 and using **temperature 0.7–0.9 for a warmer, more expressive read** ([Segmind](https://www.segmind.com/models/sarvam-bulbul-v3-tts)). No SSML or break tags were found. The price is **₹3 per 1,000 characters** ([Sarvam pricing](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)). The notes did not confirm that "Anand" is on the v3 speaker list, the per-request character limit, or the free starter credit. Check all three in the dashboard.

**(Inference)** The workaround is to render **one request per sentence or beat** and assemble the narration in the editor:

1. **Pace by passage:** 0.9–1.0 for narration, about 0.85 for the plaster, message and death beats, and 1.05–1.1 for the attack montage.
2. **Temperature and takes:** 0.7–0.8. Render 2–3 takes of every key line and keep the best, since sampling varies.
3. **Silences, added in the editor:**

   | Point in the script | Silence |
   |---|---|
   | Comma | 0.2–0.3 s |
   | "।" (full stop) | 0.5–0.7 s |
   | Beat change | about 1 s |
   | Before a reveal (the message, the shell, the citation), with music dropped | 1.5–2.5 s |
   | Chapter transition, with a music sting | 1.5 s |

4. **Emphasis:** without SSML, isolate the key word as its own short sentence ("और फिर… एक गोला।"), repeat it, or drop the pace on that clip only.
5. **Punctuation:** test whether "…" and "—" produce usable micro-pauses in v3 before relying on them.
6. **Cost:** a 1,500-word script is roughly 8,000–9,000 characters, about **₹25–30 per full render**. Several re-renders stay under ₹150.

Free fallbacks carry licence caveats. edge-tts (hi-IN Madhur) lost custom SSML in v5, and no commercial licence for it was found ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/edge-tts/6.0.1)). The ElevenLabs free tier is reportedly non-commercial ([bigvu](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plans-credits-commercial-rights-api-costs/)). AI4Bharat's IndicF5 is listed under MIT ([AIKosh](https://aikosh.indiaai.gov.in/home/models/details/aibharat_indicf5.html)). Staying on Sarvam is the cheapest licence-clean option at this scale.

The audio mix is sourced. Master to **-14 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below -1 dBTP**, and keep music **18–24 dB under the narration** for history content ([IRPR](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/guides/how-to-mix-audio-for-youtube/); [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/background-music-volume-under-voiceover)). The YouTube Audio Library is cleared for monetised videos, but terms vary track by track. Pixabay allows commercial use, yet its tracks can still draw Content ID claims that you dispute with the licence certificate. Incompetech and NCS require CC-BY credit, and missing credit is the most common cause of claims ([Thematic vs Pixabay](https://hellothematic.com/thematic-vs-pixabay/); [freetouse](https://freetouse.com/blog/no-copyright-music-subject-to-copyright-claims)).

**(Inference)** The music follows the story:

- **Cold open:** a drone.
- **Kangra childhood:** a pahadi-flavoured acoustic bed.
- **Battle:** war drums, rising only in the gaps between lines.
- **The shell:** total silence, then strings.
- **The citation:** a licensed bugle-style motif, not a ripped "Last Post".

For sound effects, use distant gunfire and wind from Freesound clips under CC0 or CC-BY only, and never run continuous gunfire under the voice. Keep every patriotic film song out. Log every track's URL, licence and credit in a sheet, and paste the credits into the description.

## Code animation becomes one layer of four

The research did not cover HyperFrames. The visual evidence still points to a hybrid that keeps the team's HTML and GSAP code for what code does best. Top war-history channels are built on **animated maps with unit arrows plus illustrated stills** ([Lilys.ai summary](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/map-animation-20260119/history-animators-maps)). In this niche, 2D and AI-animated films clearly beat talking heads: DocoHidden's Operation Jackpot reached **1.63M** views on an 11.6K-subscriber channel ([DocoHidden](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eNe0ziCRlHc)). A B-roll test found that watch-time gains appeared only when **clips of 3–8 s directly illustrated the line being spoken** ([Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/does-b-roll-increase-watch-time)). YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule names "narrated stories with only minor variations" and template slideshows as examples, and it can demonetise a whole channel ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)). A one-style ink-line piece reused across heroes would be exposed under that rule.

**(Inference)** The target mix per minute of film is 2–3 map beats, 3–5 illustrated or parallax stills, 1–2 archival images and text overlays, with a visual change every 3–6 s carried by moves within shots, not only hard cuts. The free stack:

| Layer | Tool (licence basis) | Use in this film |
|---|---|---|
| Maps and kinetic type | Existing HTML + GSAP rendered via HyperFrames (inference); base map from QGIS with Natural Earth and SRTM hillshade (background knowledge, unverified) | Kashmir overview; Baramulla to Srinagar; the Badgam company positions; arrows withdrawing at 1400; three-sided attack; citation text |
| Illustrated frames | FLUX.1 [schnell] under **Apache 2.0, commercial use allowed** ([HF](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell/discussions/55)); avoid [dev] weights | 30–40 frames in one locked style ("muted ink-and-watercolour graphic novel, 1940s Indian Army"): plaster cast, trench, village houses, mortar smoke, radio |

Skip Kling's free tier, which watermarks ([aireiter](https://aireiter.com/blog/kling-ai-free-tier-2026)) |
| Archival images | Commons 1947-war category, checking each file's tag; Indian photos are public domain 60 years after publication; PIB, DD and AIR content opened to creators in May 2025 ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1947); [Channel IAM](https://en.channeliam.com/2025/05/31/indian-public-broadcasters-offer-free-media-content)) | Real Sharma portrait; 1947 airlift photos. IWM images need a paid licence for monetised use ([IWM](https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/1060042869)) |

Keep the depiction clean of gore. YouTube's EDSA exception requires the educational context to appear in the video itself, and ads are limited when blood or injury is the focal point ([YouTube violent content policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802008?hl=en); [advertiser-friendly guidelines](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278?hl=en)).

**(Inference)** On screen this means:

- **Combat** appears at a distance or in silhouette.
- **The death** is shown through a fallen helmet and a fade to the portrait.
- **The raiders** are not dehumanised, and nothing is framed communally.
- **Kit is period-correct:** khaki drill, Mk II helmets, Lee-Enfield No.1 Mk III, no modern camouflage.
- **Labels:** archival photos carry a source tag, and colourised ones say "colourised".
- **AI disclosure:** use YouTube's altered-content label if any frame looks photoreal. The current disclosure rule was not verified in the notes.

**Captions (inference):** use Mukta, Baloo 2 or Tiro Devanagari Hindi (all OFL), 1–4 words on screen at a time, a saffron active-word highlight, and line spacing of 1.3–1.5x so matras are not clipped.

## Titles, thumbnails and algorithm targets that fit the data

YouTube ranks on **"valued watch time"**, built from clicks, watch time, satisfaction surveys, shares and likes together ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)). High CTR combined with low view duration is penalised ([ClickStudio](https://www.clickstudio.co/blog/youtube-ctr-benchmarks)). Third-party benchmarks put about half of channels at **2–10% CTR**, platform-average percentage viewed at **23.7%**, and 35–50% retention as strong for a new channel ([YTGrowth](https://ytgrowth.io/blog/youtube-ctr); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)). Title testing reportedly arrived in late 2025 ([VidAnalyze](https://vidanalyze.com/news/youtube-ab-testing-for-titles-thumbnails-2026-guide)).

## Conclusion

Story beats the earlier baseline's weakness. A sourced, specific detail such as the plaster, the 50 yards or the Gita pages outperforms bigger claims, and in this niche the bigger claims are the ones that turn out to be wrong: the 1,000 raiders, "saved Kashmir alone", the son-in-law. Accuracy here is a packaging advantage, not a constraint. It lets the film own the "well-sourced" slot that competitors leave empty, and it protects the comment section that drives satisfaction signals.

The second takeaway is structural. One 12-minute master, cut into 15 clips and released across three national dates and a school term, turns a single research effort into six weeks of daily presence. If this film's map assets, style bible and Sarvam workflow are kept as reusable infrastructure, while each script and set of visuals stays original to stay clear of the inauthentic-content rule, each later PVC film should cost a fraction of this first one. Clip 9, the Class 8 explainer, together with an English audio track, is the cheapest test of whether those two untapped audiences are real.
