## 1. Biography: birth, family, schooling, commission, WWII

### Takeaway
The core biography holds up across sources:
- Born 31 Jan 1923 at Dadh, Kangra (now Himachal Pradesh).
- Son of Maj Gen Amar Nath Sharma, in a military family.
- Schooled at Sherwood College, Nainital, then Prince of Wales Royal (Indian) Military College, Dehradun, then RMC Sandhurst.
- Commissioned 22 Feb 1942 into the 19th Hyderabad Regiment, which later became 4 Kumaon.
- Mentioned in despatches for the Arakan (Burma) campaign.

Minor conflicts: the battalion number (8th vs 9th), a stray birth date and birthplace in single sources, and the batman "Bahadur" story, which rests on a single weak source.

### Cited Findings

**Birth**
- Born 31 January 1923 at Dadh, Kangra, Punjab Province (now Himachal Pradesh), in a Dogra Brahmin family. — [Wikipedia: Somnath Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/major-somnath-sharma-birth-anniversary-all-you-need-to-know-about-the-first-recipient-of-param-vir-chakra)

**WWII service**
- Served in Burma in the Arakan campaign and was mentioned in despatches. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma); [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)

### Inferences

- For the batman anecdote, narrate it with attribution ("कहा जाता है / according to regimental lore") or drop it. Do not present it as documented.
- Use "commissioned into the 19th Hyderabad Regiment (later 4 Kumaon)" and avoid stating 8th or 9th Battalion.

### Gaps
- The London Gazette MiD entry, which would confirm the Arakan MiD date and text, was not located.
- The exact Sandhurst intake and dates were not verified. Some writers question whether a 1941–42 wartime Indian cadet went to RMC Sandhurst or to an OTS. Treat "Sandhurst" as per Wikipedia, unverified.
- Mother's name and any personal letters were not found. A letter to his parents is often quoted in Hindi videos, but **no reliable source for it was found**, so do not use it.

## 2. 1947 context: invasion, accession, airlift, 4 Kumaon's move, the plaster cast

### Takeaway
The broad sequence is documented:
1. The tribal lashkar invaded Kashmir from 22 Oct 1947.
2. Indian troops (1 Sikh first) were airlifted to Srinagar starting 27 Oct.
3. 161 Brigade's commander was wounded on 31 Oct, and Brig L.P. Sen took over on 2 Nov.
4. Sharma's company of 4 Kumaon was flown in.

Sharma's left hand/arm was in plaster from a hockey injury.

### Cited Findings
- 1 Sikh was the first battalion flown in from Delhi. Its aircraft were told to circle Srinagar airfield to confirm it had not fallen before landing. — [Military history fandom: Battle of Badgam](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam); [Tribune: "A giant leap that saved Srinagar in 1947"](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/features/a-giant-leap-that-saved-srinagar-in-1947-10123)
- Brig Katoch (161 Bde) was wounded on 31 Oct while visiting 1 Sikh at Pattan and was evacuated. Brig L.P. Sen took over the brigade on 2 Nov. — [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- **The plaster cast:**
  - Sharma's **left hand** was in a plaster cast from a hockey-field injury, yet he insisted on going with his company. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)
  - ThePrint's excerpt (Ian Cardozo) calls it an "arm fracture". — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/ambushed-injured-in-kashmir-how-major-somnath-sharma-earned-a-param-vir-chakra/185018/)
  - One blog says right arm, which conflicts with the others. — [search summary of blogs incl. cestmoizblog](https://cestmoizblog.com/2017/11/03/maj-somnath-sharma-pvc-p-and-the-battle-of-badgam/)
- The Organiser (2026) says the cast left him unable to fire a rifle, so he took to distributing magazines and ammunition under mortar fire. — [Organiser, 31 Jan 2026](https://organiser.org/2026/01/31/337819/bharat/birth-anniversary-of-major-somnath-sharma-the-man-the-myth-the-plaster-cast/)
  - Organiser is a partisan publication with a mixed accuracy record, but the "filling magazines" detail is widely repeated (see Better India and Wikipedia pointers).
- ThePrint's excerpt headline says he "led his battalion to Budgam in Kashmir in October 1947". This is loose. He commanded a company, not the battalion, and Badgam was on 3 Nov. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/ambushed-injured-in-kashmir-how-major-somnath-sharma-earned-a-param-vir-chakra/185018/)

### Inferences
- Phrase it as "his left arm/hand was in plaster after a hockey injury; he persuaded his CO to let him go." The "persuaded" framing is common but its primary source was not seen.
- Say "fracture" only with the Cardozo attribution.

### Gaps
- 22 Oct (invasion start), 26 Oct (Instrument of Accession) and 27 Oct (first airlift, now Infantry Day) are standard historical dates, but **no source fetched in this session** explicitly confirmed them. Verify them with an Indian Army or PIB page before narration.
- The exact date the D Company of 4 Kumaon landed at Srinagar was not verified. Wikipedia reportedly says 31 Oct, but the page was not readable.
- No primary source (such as Sen's *Slender Was the Thread*, 1969) was accessible to confirm the CO conversation about the plaster.

## 3. The Battle of Badgam, 3 November 1947

### Takeaway
On 3 Nov 1947 a patrol of two (some say three) companies went to Badgam to check for raiders approaching Srinagar airfield. The force was 4 Kumaon's A and D Companies, plus a 1 (Para) Kumaon company per some accounts.

The sequence:
1. There was no contact early on, and companies began falling back around 1400 hrs.
2. D Company under Sharma stayed.
3. At about 1435 hrs it came under fire from houses in Badgam village.
4. A lashkar of roughly 500–1,000 men, most often given as about 700, attacked from three sides. Many had gathered posing as local villagers.

Sharma was killed by a mortar explosion. Losses were Sharma, one JCO (Subedar Prem Singh Mehta) and about 20 other ranks. The stand delayed the raiders long enough for reinforcements (1 Punjab, a 1 Kumaon relief company) to block the approach to the airfield.

### Cited Findings

**The plan and forces**
- The patrol was to comprise two companies of 4 Kumaon reinforced by a company of 1 Kumaon. If no contact was made, the 4 Kumaon companies would fall back one by one, vacating Badgam by 1400 hrs. — [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam, via search summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- Its task was to search the area around Badgam, and between Badgam and Magam, for infiltrating raiders. — [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- **Conflict on force size.** Accounts give:
  - "a company of 4 Kumaon";
  - "two companies, 'A' and 'D', of 4 Kumaon under Major Somnath Sharma, and a company of 1 Para Kumaon under Captain Ronald Wood";
  - "three companies". — [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam); [gallantwarriors.com](https://gallantwarriors.com/the-battle-of-badgam-the-bravest-last-man-standing-battle-ever-fought/); [Wikipedia: Somnath Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)
- Sharma commanded D Company, 4 Kumaon. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)
- He reached Badgam at first light on 3 Nov and had his troops take up a fighting position immediately. — [CLAWS Scholar Warrior, Sameer Mallya, 2015](https://archive.claws.co.in/images/journals_doc/95557802_SameerMallya.pdf)

**Timeline**
- At 1400 hrs Sharma reported that A Company had moved back towards the airfield. Wikipedia's Sharma article says two of the three companies returned to Srinagar at 2 pm. — [search summary citing Military history fandom and Wikipedia](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- At 1435 hrs D Company came under fire from some houses in Badgam village. — [Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/) / [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam), per search summary
- The company was surrounded on three sides and took heavy casualties from mortar fire. — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/73888/major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-recipient/); [Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/)

**The "villagers" deception**
- The raiders had been arriving in Badgam in small groups to conceal their movement, and planned to attack once about 1,000 had gathered. — [CLAWS, Mallya](https://archive.claws.co.in/images/journals_doc/95557802_SameerMallya.pdf)
- Popular accounts describe raiders "disguised and posing as Kashmiri villagers with their weapons hidden under their loose cloaks". — [bioconblog / soldierstoryofkashmir, per search summary](https://www.soldierstoryofkashmir.com/post/saviours-of-kashmir-major-somnath-sharma-pvc)
- The "weapons under cloaks" image is a popular embellishment. The academic line is "arrived in small groups to conceal movement".

**Enemy strength (conflicting)**
- About 700, with small arms, mortars and heavy automatics. — [CLAWS Mallya / Wikipedia, per search summary](https://archive.claws.co.in/images/journals_doc/95557802_SameerMallya.pdf)
- About 500, "led by Pakistani officers". — [search summary of popular accounts](https://bioconblog.com/2020/08/13/valor-folklore-the-hero-of-badgam/)
- About 1,000 was the planned gathering strength. — [CLAWS Mallya](https://archive.claws.co.in/images/journals_doc/95557802_SameerMallya.pdf)
- Some write "500–1,000". — [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)

**The last message (wording varies)**
- Most quoted version: "The enemy are only 50 yards from us. We are heavily outnumbered. We are under devastating fire. I shall not withdraw an inch but will fight to the last man and the last round." — widely repeated, e.g. [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/73888/major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-recipient/); [Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/)
- Shorter variant: "The enemy are only 50 yards from us. I shall not withdraw an inch but will fight to our last man and our last round." Described as received at Brigade HQ "a few moments before he was killed". — [indiaofthepast.org](https://www.indiaofthepast.org/contribute-memories/read-contributions/armed-forces-martyrs/522-major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-awardee)
- Wikipedia (Battle of Badgam) variant: "I will not withdraw one inch but fight to the last man last round." — [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- **Wording conflict:**
  - All versions share the core: "50 yards", "not withdraw an inch", "last man, last round".
  - "We are heavily outnumbered / under devastating fire" appears in some versions only.
  - No primary log entry (war diary) was found.
  - No source confirmed the time the message was received. The 1435 hrs time is the start of firing, not the message.

**Death**
- A mortar shell landed near him and caused an explosion (accounts say on the ammunition he was handling), killing him. — [search summary of ThePrint/others](https://theprint.in/pageturner/excerpt/ambushed-injured-in-kashmir-how-major-somnath-sharma-earned-a-param-vir-chakra/185018/); [Organiser, 6 Nov 2026](https://organiser.org/2026/11/06/424665/bharat/major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-awardee-hero-who-stood-tall-amid-mortar-fire-in-battle-of-budgam/)

**Indian casualties**
- Major Sharma, one JCO and 20 other ranks of D Company were killed. Other accounts name Subedar Prem Singh Mehta as the JCO and also list Sepoy Dewan Singh among the dead. — [Wikipedia: Battle of Badgam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam); [Military history fandom](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- Ambiguity: it is unclear whether Dewan Singh is counted within the "20 other ranks" or in addition to them.

**Enemy casualties**
- "When Badgam was recaptured... the bodies of over 300 tribesmen were counted." — [search summary of popular/fandom accounts](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- This rests on a single strand of sources, so attribute it if used.

**Relief and outcome**
- A relief company from 1 Kumaon reached Badgam after D Company's position had been overrun. — [Wikipedia: Somnath Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)
- In the evening, 1 Punjab moved to Badgam and occupied blocking positions to secure the airfield. 1 Punjab later recaptured Badgam, and the lashkar abandoned its advance. — [Military history fandom / Wikipedia, per search summary](https://military-history.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_of_Badgam)
- Some accounts say the stand "delayed the enemy for about six hours", allowing reinforcements to hold Humhama (the airfield area). One source attributes this to the official citation. — [Unsung Champions](https://unsungchampions.com/major-somnath-sharma/)

**Recovery and identification**
- His body was recovered about three days later and was disfigured beyond recognition. It was identified by his pistol holster (some say pistol) and a few pages of the Bhagavad Gita in his breast pocket. — [Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/); [ssbpsych](https://www.ssbpsych.com/indian-armed-forces/major-somnath-sharma-param-vir-chakra/); [hindupost](https://hindupost.in/history/frontline-hero-major-somnath-sharma-and-the-battle-of-badgam/)
- Secondary sources only. "Leather holster" and "pistol" both appear.

### Inferences
- **Safe narration:** "about 700 raiders" (most-cited, and used by the CLAWS paper per search summary). Alternatively say "several hundred, by some estimates up to a thousand".
- **Safe message wording:** give the core sentence — "The enemy are only 50 yards from us... I shall not withdraw an inch but will fight to the last man and the last round." The full four-sentence version is the most widely quoted and is acceptable if introduced as "his last message, as it is recorded in regimental accounts".
- **On what the stand achieved:**
  - Do not claim on-screen that Sharma's company "alone saved Srinagar".
  - Better: "bought crucial hours for reinforcements to block the road to the airfield, which kept India's air bridge open".
  - The overrunning of D Company and the later 1 Punjab and 1 Sikh actions are part of the picture. Brig Sen's brigade, including 1 Sikh and later the armoured cars at Shalateng (7 Nov), finally broke the lashkar.

### Gaps
- No war diary or official history (MoD, *Operations in Jammu & Kashmir 1947–48*) text was accessible to confirm timings, strengths or the message log.
- D Company's strength (often "about 90–100 men") was **not found in any source this session**. Do not state a number.
- Captain Ronald Wood's role is uncertain. He appears in only one search-summarised page, as commanding a 1 Para Kumaon company. His fate and any award were not found.
- No reliable source was found for "1 Sikh reinforcements arriving because of Badgam" specifically. The documented relief force is 1 Kumaon's company and then 1 Punjab.

## 4. The Param Vir Chakra: gazette, citation, first award, the Khanolkar connection

### Takeaway
- Sharma's PVC was the first ever awarded. It was posthumous and gazetted on **21 June 1950** (notification 2 Pres/50) for 3 Nov 1947.
- The PVC was instituted on 26 Jan 1950 with retrospective effect from 15 Aug 1947.
- The design story is real. Savitri Khanolkar (born Eve Yvonne Maday de Maros) designed the medal, and her daughter Kumudini married Somnath's brother. That makes him her daughter's brother-in-law, **not her son-in-law**, which some headlines get wrong.

### Cited Findings
- PVC instituted 26 January 1950 with retrospective effect from 15 August 1947. First awards published in the Gazette of India on 21 June 1950. — [Wikipedia: Param Vir Chakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Param_Vir_Chakra); [Wikipedia: Somnath Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somnath_Sharma)
- Gazette notification "2 Pres/50, 21.6.50". — [Honourpoint](https://honourpoint.in/profile/major-somnath-sharma-pvc-2/)
- **Citation text:** the full verbatim citation was not retrieved.
  - Fragments in secondary sources say his company "was ordered on a fighting patrol to Badgam in the Kashmir Valley on 3 November 1947". — [incredibleindia blog](https://incredibleindia2019863669658.wordpress.com/2020/07/06/first-recipient-of-param-vir-chakra-major-somnath-sharma/)
  - They close with words that his "example of courage... [has] seldom been equalled in the history of the Indian Army". — [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/73888/major-somnath-sharma-first-param-vir-chakra-recipient/); [Republic World](https://www.republicworld.com/defence/indian-armed-forces/how-maj-somnath-sharma-s-legendary-last-stand-made-him-indias-1st-param-vir)
  - The two transcriptions differ in spelling ("equaled"/"equalled"), so neither is verbatim-verified.
- **Savitri Khanolkar**
  - She was born Eve Yvonne Maday de Maros in Neuchâtel, Switzerland, and married Indian Army officer Vikram Ramji Khanolkar. — [Wikipedia: Savitri Khanolkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitri_Khanolkar); [The Rising India](https://therisingindia.in/history-mystery/who-designed-indias-param-vir-chakra/)
  - Her elder daughter Kumudini married into Sharma's family. Somnath was Kumudini's brother-in-law. — [Wikipedia: Savitri Khanolkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitri_Khanolkar)
  - Kumudini's husband is named as Lt Gen Surinder (Surindra) Nath Sharma, later Engineer-in-Chief. This comes from a lower-quality aggregator, but it is consistent with the Wikipedia entry on S.N. Sharma. — [WikiBio](https://wikibio.in/savitri-khanolkar/); [Wikipedia: Surindra Nath Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surindra_Nath_Sharma)
  - **Wrong claim to avoid:** an india.com headline says "her son-in-law became its first recipient". — [india.com](https://www.india.com/viral/who-is-savitri-khanolkar-foreign-woman-who-designed-indias-param-vir-chakra-her-son-in-law-became-its-first-recipient-his-name-is-major-somnath-sharma-battle-of-badgam-8013982/)

### Inferences
- The poignant irony is real and usable: "the medal designed by a woman whose daughter had married into his family was first awarded to him, after his death." Avoid "son-in-law".
- The claim that the design was finalised before or after Badgam is not established here. Don't imply she designed it "for him".

### Gaps
- The verbatim official citation is needed from the Gazette of India (21 June 1950), gallantryawards.gov.in or the National War Memorial page (https://nationalwarmemorial.org/Bharat/Somnath_Sharma). It could not be fetched.
- Kumudini's marriage date was not found.

## 6. Disputed or commonly wrong claims (popular videos and articles)

### Takeaway
Most embellishment concerns:
- enemy numbers;
- the message wording;
- "he single-handedly saved Kashmir/Srinagar";
- the batman story;
- which arm was in plaster;
- the "son-in-law" Khanolkar error;
- the "led his battalion" phrasing.
